# Chip icons: the four objects, in four hi-fi styles

The four cut-out objects that sit ON the library chips — the heart, the stack of
records, the loop, the figure at the decks — written in the same hi-fi material
language as the textures, so an object and the backing behind it come from one
world rather than two.

## The brief here INVERTS the texture brief

A texture has to be dark enough for white type to sit on it. These objects sit
*on* that texture, with a darkening wash between, so **here the object is the
light**: it has to be clearly brighter than its ground and hold together as one
shape rather than dissolving into it.

## The constraint that actually decides these

| | |
|---|---|
| Placement | right **74%** of the chip, full height, bleeding **8%** off the right edge |
| Draw | `object-fit: contain`, 95% opacity |
| **Real display size** | about **139 × 80 px** on a phone |

That last number is the brief. At 139 × 80, anything under roughly 4% of the
frame is invisible — which is a genuine problem for four materials that are
*entirely about surface and light*. Brushed grain, lacquer reflections, dial
graduations: all exactly what disappears first.

So every prompt is written **bold silhouette over detail**, and each style
carries a note on how its material was kept legible at that size. Read those
notes before generating — they are where the real thinking is.

Two of the four are tilted by the app (−8° on Liked, +7° on All songs), so a
slightly informal composition suits them. **Do not build a tilt into the image.**

## Append to every prompt

> 512×512 PNG, transparent background. One object, centred, filling most of the
> frame with a little clear margin. Bright enough to read on a near-black
> textured ground. One silhouette — no scattered elements, no scene, no ground
> plane, no cast shadow, no frame, no border. No text, no letters, no numerals,
> no logos. Bold shape over fine detail.

## Not included

**Recent** and **New Playlist** are deliberately absent. Those two went back to
plain line glyphs because they are *controls* — one opens a view, one opens a
dialog — and a control wearing a face competes with the destinations around it.
Giving them hi-fi objects would undo that on purpose.

## 1. Valve glow

*I kept the glow structural rather than decorative — one closed, chunky primary form per tile whose filament runs along the outline itself, so at 139x80 the amber light reads as the shape's own bright body against black, while the mica, getter flash and chassis metal are only there to give the big form weight and never carry the read.*


**Liked — the heart**

> A heart blown from a single smoked-glass vacuum tube envelope, its amber filament coiled inside tracing the whole heart outline in hot orange-pink light so the shape glows from within. Mirror-silver getter flash frosts the upper lobes; a squat nickel valve base caps the point. Thick glass walls carry a bright rim highlight; the internal glow bleeds warm through the smoke.


**All songs — the stack of records**

> A leaning stack of flat dark-chassis record sleeves, each edge glowing a thin amber filament line like heater wire, one round mica-and-glass disc sliding out from the middle and blazing warm orange from inside. Blue getter flash silvers the disc's upper curve; brushed steel corners catch cold highlights. Heavy simple silhouette: solid slab, one protruding circle.


**On repeat — the loop**

> A thick closed loop of bent glass tubing, two chasing arrowheads formed where the ends overlap, an amber filament running continuously inside so the whole ring burns bright orange. It encircles a dark smoked-glass disc with a blue getter-flash sheen and a small nickel centre boss. Fat even tube walls, wide gaps, arrowheads solid and blunt.


**DJ — the figure at the decks**

> A broad-shouldered figure behind two decks, head-on: the helmet is a domed glass valve envelope with an amber filament glowing inside and a blue getter-flash crown, shoulders and arms cast in dark chassis steel with warm light seeping from the seams. Both hands rest on two round platters that burn orange like heated cathodes, a low mixer slab between them.


## 2. Machined metal

*I inverted the usual charcoal-anodised look so the metal itself is bright bead-blasted silver and the anodising is pushed into recesses only, then let a wide chamfer run the entire outline of each object so a single raked highlight draws a continuous bright edge — the shape survives as one solid, fat-limbed mass even when the brushing, knurling and turning marks blur away at thumbnail size.*


**Liked — the heart**

> A single heart milled from a solid aluminium billet, bright bead-blasted silver, its whole outline carried by a wide polished chamfer that catches a raked cool highlight so the edge glows white against dark. The broad face is turned in overlapping jewelled swirls, split by one deep channel of crimson anodising and two countersunk hex screws. Fat, rounded, symmetrical, heavy solid mass, no thin points.


**All songs — the stack of records**

> A leaning stack of square machined plates, like record sleeves cut from brushed aluminium, each edge chamfered to a bright polished line; one thick turned disc slides out from the middle, its concentric machining rings catching a raked cool light, a crimson anodised hub at the centre. Charcoal anodised faces, bright silver edges, countersunk bolts at the corners. One compact leaning wedge of a silhouette.


**On repeat — the loop**

> A repeat loop milled from one piece of aluminium: a thick closed ring broken by two fat chasing arrowheads, each arm a solid bar with wide chamfers and a knurled outer band, bright bead-blasted silver lit by raked cool highlights. The ring encloses a recessed charcoal anodised disc turned in fine concentric rings, so the bright loop stands clear and unbroken.


**DJ — the figure at the decks**

> A performer machined from solid aluminium, seen head-on: a smooth domed helmet with a slotted crimson anodised visor, wide angled shoulders, hands resting on two thick turned platters that flank a knurled mixer block with chamfered faders. Bright bead-blasted silver bodies, charcoal anodised deck plinths, raked cool highlights along every polished chamfer. Broad, blocky, symmetrical, one heavy connected mass.


## 3. Piano gloss

*I inverted the obvious reading of the material — the lacquer body is piano-WHITE rather than black so the object is the bright thing on a dark tile, black mirror gloss survives only as one or two small inset faces, and every highlight is a single broad sweeping band across a whole mass instead of scattered pinpoint speculars, so the shape holds as one glowing silhouette at 139x80.*


**Liked — the heart**

> A single fat heart moulded from thick piano-white lacquer, its curves polished to a mirror: one broad soft-box highlight sweeps across the whole upper lobe, a cool blue-white edge glances the rim, and warm crimson-pink light pools in the cleft and along the underside. The gloss is deep and wet, faint dust specks caught in the sheen. Massive simple form, plump and heavy.


**All songs — the stack of records**

> A leaning stack of square record sleeves moulded in glossy piano-white lacquer, each slab edge catching a long specular streak, one black mirror-gloss disc sliding out and flaring a bright crescent of light. Deep reflections pool between the slabs; crimson-pink bounce warms the lowest edges. Chunky simplified blocks, thick as lacquered piano keys, dust glinting on the top face.


**On repeat — the loop**

> Two thick arrows chasing each other around a closed loop, moulded from glossy piano-white lacquer as one continuous ribbon with heavy blunt arrowheads. A broad soft-box highlight runs along the whole band; the black mirror-gloss record inside it reflects a single pale crescent. Crimson-pink glow warms the underside of the ring. Wide open centre, unmistakable circular loop, dust in the sheen.


**DJ — the figure at the decks**

> A helmeted figure behind two turntables and a mixer, seen head-on, the whole group moulded as one glossy piano-white lacquer object. The domed visor is black mirror gloss with a single wide highlight arc; shoulders, platters and console catch long soft-box streaks. Crimson-pink light rims the arms and helmet edge. Broad simplified masses, hands on the decks, no small parts.


## 4. Meters and lamps

*I made the glowing cream dial face the silhouette itself rather than a detail on it, and wrapped every mass in a thick bright chrome bezel, so at 139x80 the object still reads as one solid lit shape with a hard rim while the graduations, lamps and needles quietly drop away.*


**Liked — the heart**

> A single heart-shaped VU meter, thick polished chrome bezel following the whole heart outline, cream printed scale glowing warm ivory from behind the glass, one black needle swung hard right into a wide crimson overload sector. Fat even bezel weight, generous rounded lobes, a small red pilot lamp at the point. The lit dial face is the entire shape, no surrounding fascia.


**All songs — the stack of records**

> A leaning stack of black instrument modules, each edge caught in bright chrome and each face a glowing cream VU window with a crimson sector at its right; the top module tips and a round backlit dial face slides out sideways like a disc leaving its sleeve. Warm ivory light spills from every window. Solid rectangular mass, one bright circle escaping it.


**On repeat — the loop**

> A closed loop built as a curved backlit VU scale: a thick chrome-bezelled ring, cream printed graduations glowing from behind it, broken into two chasing arcs that finish in heavy solid arrowheads. One tail crosses a crimson overload segment; a small pilot lamp burns at each arrowhead. Fat even stroke, wide open centre, unmistakably a repeat symbol.


**DJ — the figure at the decks**

> A performer head-on, assembled from instrument parts: the helmet a round chrome-bezelled meter with a glowing cream dial face and one black needle, the shoulders a black fascia plate rimmed in bright chrome. Below, two large backlit circular VU dials stand in for turntables with a short crimson fader between them and a row of pilot lamps. Chunky, symmetrical, ivory-lit.
